M0: Why the Body Needs Control

Read, look, then check yourself.

Life Responds to Change

Living things do not just move for no reason. They notice what is happening and then react.

The chapter starts with familiar examples like a cat chasing a mouse, a child pulling away from heat, and a plant turning toward sunlight.

  • Movement can be a response to the environment.
  • The response is controlled, not random.
  • Different situations need different actions.
Big idea: control and coordination help an organism choose the right response at the right time.

How a Nerve Message Travels

Nerve cells carry information as electrical impulses. The message starts at the dendrite, moves through the cell body, goes along the axon, and crosses the synapse to the next cell.

Receptors in sense organs notice the change first. Gustatory receptors sense taste, and olfactory receptors sense smell.

  • Receptors detect the stimulus.
  • A neuron carries the impulse.
  • The synapse passes the message on.

M1: Reflexes and the Brain

Read, look, then check yourself.

Reflex Actions Are Super Fast

A reflex action is a quick, automatic response to something that could hurt you. Touching a hot pan is the classic example.

The signal uses a reflex arc, and the spinal cord helps the body react quickly.

  • Reflex actions are automatic.
  • They protect the body from danger.
  • The spinal cord helps make the response fast.

The Brain Keeps the Plan Together

The brain helps control thinking, balance, memory, and many other actions. The cerebrum helps with thinking, the cerebellum helps with balance, and the medulla handles life-support jobs.

It is protected by the skull, meninges, and a fluid cushion around it.

  • Cerebrum: thinking and learning
  • Cerebellum: balance and movement
  • Medulla: breathing and heartbeat control

M2: Plants Respond Too

Read, look, then check yourself.

The Sensitive Plant Responds Right Away

Plants also respond to the world around them. The sensitive plant folds its leaves when touched.

This is an immediate response, but it is not the same as growth.

  • Touch can trigger a quick plant response.
  • Some plant movements are not growth based.
  • Plants still need coordination.

Growth Movements Are Directional

Some plant movements happen because of growth. A shoot bends toward light, and roots grow downward into the soil. These are called tropisms.

Plants may look still, but they are constantly adjusting their growth to match the environment.

  • Phototropism: growth in response to light
  • Geotropism: growth in response to gravity
  • Growth movements are slow but important

M3: Hormones Do the Long-Range Work

Read, look, then check yourself.

Hormones in Animals

Hormones are chemical messengers. They move through the blood and tell another part of the body what to do.

Growth hormone helps the body grow, insulin helps control blood sugar, and adrenaline helps you react in an emergency.

  • Growth hormone supports growth.
  • Insulin controls blood sugar.
  • Adrenaline prepares the body for danger.

Feedback Keeps Hormones Balanced

The nervous system and hormone system work together. The nervous system is fast and precise. Hormones are slower but useful for long-range control.

Plants also use chemical signals to coordinate growth and response.

Final memory line: nerves send quick messages, hormones send long-range messages, and both help the body stay organized.
